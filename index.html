Behaviour

What the geometry above is actually doing.

Standing wave along the arm

The arm is a quarter-wave resonator shorted at one end. Current peaks at the short pin and falls to zero at the open tip; voltage does the opposite. The feed taps that standing wave — and where it taps sets the impedance the radio sees. Drag the short pin and watch the tap marker slide along the curve while the curve itself keeps its shape.

Current I(d) Voltage V(d)

Idealized lossless standing wave, I(d) = cos(π/2 · d/L). A teaching model, not an EM solve — no impedance in ohms is claimed.

Resonant length vs frequency

Why high-frequency radios get small antennas. The curve is L = k · λ/4 across the whole tuning range; the shaded band marks frequencies whose arm is too long to fold into your current board.

How this works

        

          
A quarter-wavelength of copper resonates at the frequency you want. At 915 MHz that is about
            78 mm — far too long for a small board, so the trace is folded back on itself.
            Folding does not change the electrical length: what matters is the total distance travelled
            along the copper, not its shape.

          
The short pin ties one end of that trace to ground. The feed pad taps
            into it a few millimetres away. Sliding the tap point up and down the trace changes the
            impedance the radio sees, without touching the resonant length — which is why an inverted-F
            can be matched without a tuning capacitor.

          
The correction factor k = 0.95 shortens the theoretical quarter-wave slightly
            to account for fringing at the open end. It is empirical, and it is exposed in the settings
            rather than buried in the code.

        

      

      

        
The maths, in full

        

          
wavelength   λ = c / f

          
arm length   L = k · λ / 4   (k = 0.95, empirical)

          
folded path length = L, regardless of fold count

        

        

          
No impedance or SWR figure is shown anywhere in this tool. An honest number needs a real
            electromagnetic solver; printing a plausible-looking one would be worse than printing none.
            Match quality is described in words only.

          
Substrate permittivity and thickness are recorded in the exported files but do not enter the
            length formula — k absorbs those effects in this model.
